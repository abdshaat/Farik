# Forecasting

A forecast is a reasoned guess, shown with its reasoning. It is worth what a reader can check.

## 1. Start from what happened

Read the last three months, or the last three sprints when the team plans in sprints, from the
books and from `catervas_read_costs`. Forecast the next three of the same kind. When there are fewer
than three, say so and forecast fewer.

## 2. Name the method

Say which method you used and why: the average of the three when the figures are steady, or the
trend when they are rising or falling steadily. Do not use a method you cannot explain in a
sentence.

## 3. A range, not one figure

Give a low and a high for each period, and what the figure depends on. One number looks more sure
than it is.

## 4. Put the assumptions in their own sheet

Write the assumptions on their own sheet of `forecast.xlsx`, one to a row: what you assumed, the
number you used, and what would make it wrong. Write the forecast beside them as values, never
formulas that reach outside the workbook.

## 5. Date it and name its inputs

Never write a forecast without the date you made it and the sources and dates of every input.

## 6. Say what you left out

If a cost or a price is not in the books, such as a plan that is about to change, say so in the
note rather than leave the reader to find it.
