# Unit economics and pricing

Unit economics say whether one customer is worth having. Work them out from the books, not from
what the user hopes.

## 1. Revenue per customer

Divide the revenue of a period by its customers, and say how you counted a customer and which
period you used. Stripe gives the totals; do not read a customer's own details to count them.

## 2. Cost per customer

Add what the product costs to serve one customer: the team's AI spending that belongs to the
product's running, the payment fees, hosting and the other costs in the books. The AI spending
and the fees are always included. Say which costs you left out and why.

## 3. Gross margin and payback

Gross margin is revenue less those costs, as a share of revenue. Payback is how many months of
margin repay what it cost to win the customer. Show the figures each is made from.

## 4. A price is a recommendation

When asked whether a price works, answer with the numbers: the margin now, the margin at the
price asked about, and what would have to be true for it to pay. Recommend, in a few plain
sentences. You never change a price, a plan or anything at Stripe.

## 5. Say how sure you are

A small number of customers makes every figure rough. Say how many there were, and say so when
the answer could change with one more customer.
