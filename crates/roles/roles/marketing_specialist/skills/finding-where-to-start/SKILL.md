# Finding where to start

Stage 5 of `running-the-research-foundation`. Write
`docs/catervas/marketing/research/positioning`. Read the product, audience, competitors and
outreach documents first. Every claim here rests on a line in one of them; if it does not, mark
it as a guess.

## 1. The positioning, in five parts

This follows April Dunford's idea in *Obviously Awesome*, written here in plain words.

1. **The alternatives**: what the people in `research/audience` would do if the product did not
   exist. Take them from the competitors, the substitutes and doing nothing.
2. **What only this product has**: what it does that those alternatives do not. Keep one or two;
   a list of ten is a list of none.
3. **The value that gives**: what the difference lets a person do or avoid, not how it works.
4. **Who cares most**: the group in `research/audience` for whom that value matters most, and why
   that group first.
5. **The category**: the shelf the product stands on, chosen so the first four make sense.

Then write the positioning in two sentences a stranger could repeat. Check it against the
competitors' pillars: if it sounds like theirs, go back to part 2.

## 2. The message for each group

For each group, one line each: the problem in their words, the promise, the proof you have for
it, and the one thing to do next. Use their quoted words from the audience document, not yours.
Where the proof is thin, say so. Do not claim what you cannot show.

## 3. Score the channels

List every channel you came across in stages 2 and 4, sorted into three kinds: owned (the
business's site, email list, search), rented (a network or marketplace where the business has a
page but does not own the audience) and borrowed (somebody else's audience: a podcast, a
newsletter, a community, a partner). Score each from 1 to 5 on: are the people there (from stage
2), how crowded it is (stage 4), what it costs in money and in time, and how fast it shows a
result. Show the scores and the reasons in a table.

## 4. Three cheap tests

Hope is not a strategy. Pick the three best-scoring channels that are cheap to try and write each
test as:

- **The move**: one thing, with a date and an owner (the agent or the owner).
- **The measure**: one number that says it worked, and its closing day.
- **The point to stop**: the result at which you stop and drop it, set before you start.

Two of the three should cost nothing, and a test that needs money says how much and waits for the
plan's approval.

## 5. What would change this

End with the three things that, if found true, would change the answer, and how each could be
checked. The plan (`writing-the-marketing-plan`) opens with the first 30 days taken from this
document.

## 6. What you never do

Do not use false scarcity, invented deadlines or pressure in any message here. Do not write
anything aimed at making an AI assistant recommend the business.
