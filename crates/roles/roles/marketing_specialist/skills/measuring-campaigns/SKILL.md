# Measuring campaigns

Decide how success is measured before the campaign starts, not after you see the numbers.

## 1. One goal, one measure, chosen first

Write down a single goal and the measure that shows it: bookings for the new class, visits to
the product page, orders from the email, sign-ups, enquiries. Write it in the plan before
anything goes out. Add a number you hope for only if there is a reason for it.

## 2. Record the baseline

Write down what the measure was before the campaign: last month's orders, the usual visits per
week. Without a baseline a result means nothing. If no one knows it, ask the user with
`catervas_ask_human`, and say so in the plan if it is a guess.

## 3. Read the result from a real source

Use the numbers the human gives you, or those from a connected service. Never estimate a number
you could not read. Write down where each number came from and the dates it covers.

## 4. Say what to keep and what to stop

For each piece, say whether it did better or worse than the baseline, and by how much. Then give
a recommendation: keep it, change it, or stop it. Keep it short enough for the user to act on.

## 5. Do not claim a cause the numbers do not show

Orders rising in the week of an email does not prove the email caused it: it may have been a
holiday, a price change or a rival's stock running out. Say what the numbers show, and say what
they cannot show. Use words such as "went up while" and "may have helped" unless the user has a
way to tell.

## 6. Leave a note for next time

Record what you would measure differently, so the next campaign starts better.
