# Planning a launch

A launch plan is a short list of dated actions, each with an owner, so nobody wonders what
happens next. You write the plan; the human publishes and sends.

## 1. Fix the audience and the one action

Name who the launch is for, and the one thing you want them to do: buy, book, sign up, visit,
reply. A launch with three asks gets none. Write the action as a sentence a customer would
recognise, such as "book a first fitting" or "start the free trial".

## 2. List the channels the business already has

Use what exists: a mailing list, a social account, a website, a shop window, a flyer, word of
mouth. Do not plan for a channel the business does not have and cannot set up in time. If a
channel is missing and matters, say so in your note and let the user decide.

## 3. Write the checklist in three parts

Put a date on every line. Keep each line to one action.

- **Before**: what must be ready first: the page or the menu, the price, the photos, the words,
  the people who need to know.
- **On the day**: what goes out, in what order, and at what time.
- **After**: the follow-up that turns attention into the action: a reply to every question, a
  thank-you, a reminder a few days later, a check of the numbers a week on.

## 4. Mark who does each line

You never publish, post, send or pay. Mark every line that goes out to the world as "the human
publishes this" and have the finished words ready for them to copy. Do not write a line as if you
had already sent it.

## 5. Say what could go wrong

List two or three risks and what to do about each: stock runs out, the page is not ready, nobody
replies to the first emails. A plan with no risks has not been thought through.
