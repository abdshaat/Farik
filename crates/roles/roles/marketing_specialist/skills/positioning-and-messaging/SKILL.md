# Positioning and messaging

Positioning is the choice of what to say first, and to whom. Make it before you write a word of
copy, and write it down so the user can disagree with it.

## 1. Answer five questions

- **Who is it for?** One kind of person, described by their situation, not by their age. A
  neighbourhood bakery's customer is "someone who wants fresh bread on the way home". A new app's
  is "a small team that tracks work in spreadsheets".
- **What problem do they have, in their words?** Use the words customers use, from reviews,
  emails and what the user pastes. Do not use the team's own terms.
- **What do they do about it now?** The alternative is often a habit or a rival, not nothing.
- **What is the one thing that is different?** One, not five. If you cannot say it in a sentence,
  you have not found it.
- **What is the proof?** A number, a fact, a review or a demonstration the user can show.

If the user can answer one of these and you cannot find it in `docs/catervas/product/` or your own folder, ask with
`catervas_ask_human`. Do not fill the gap with a guess.

## 2. Write the positioning

Write one statement in this shape: "For (who), who (problem), (the business) is (what it is) that
(the difference), unlike (the alternative)." Then write three messages. Each message is one
sentence for a customer, with the proof that backs it beside it.

## 3. No claim without a source

Every claim names where it comes from: a document in `docs/catervas/product/` or `docs/catervas/marketing/`, a page the user pasted, a source you
read and the day you read it. If you cannot name one, mark the sentence as a guess for the user to
confirm, or take it out. Never invent a number, a customer or a quote.

## 4. Check it against the alternative

Read the three messages as a customer who already uses the alternative. If they would say "mine
does that too", the message is not a difference. Rewrite it or drop it.
