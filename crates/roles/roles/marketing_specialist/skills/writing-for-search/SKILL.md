# Writing for search

People find a page when its words match what they type. Your job is to use their words, for one
topic at a time, without making the page worse to read.

## 1. Start from the words people search for

Find them from research: what customers write in reviews and emails, what the user pastes, the
suggestions a search engine shows, and what competitors' pages are named. Do not invent a phrase
and assume people use it. Write the list down with where each phrase came from.

## 2. One topic per page

Choose one main phrase for each page and let the page answer that question completely. If two
pages want the same phrase, merge them or give each its own question. A shop's page for "leather
wallets" should not also try to be the page for "leather belts".

## 3. Titles and descriptions

- The page title says what the page is, with the main phrase near the front. Keep it under 60
  characters so it is not cut off in results.
- The description is one or two sentences that make a person want to click, under 155
  characters.
- Write each one by hand. Two pages never share a title.

## 4. Headings answer the question

Make the headings the questions a customer has, in their words, and answer each directly in the
first sentence beneath it. A reader who only scans the headings should still learn the answer.

## 5. No stuffing

Use the phrase where it fits naturally, and use plain variants of it. Repeating it to hit a count
makes the page worse and does not help. If a sentence reads oddly aloud, rewrite it.

## 6. For a shop or a service

Write product names and attributes the way customers write them: the colour, the size, the
material, the place and the use ("waterproof walking boots, women's, size 6"). Put the plain
facts a buyer needs, price, delivery, opening hours and area served, in text on the page, not
only in a picture.
