# Checking product safety

Any physical product can be recalled or sold without meeting the rules where it is sold. Check
both before you recommend it, whether it is a baby car mirror, an electrical charger or a box of
brake pads.

## 1. Recalls first

Look for a recall of the product, of its maker's model and of the maker's other products, on an
official agency's own public page:
- in the United States, the Consumer Product Safety Commission, at `cpsc.gov`;
- the maker's own recall or safety-notice page;
- in another country, that country's consumer safety agency.

None of these is on Farik's list of sites you may read. Ask for the one you need before you end
your turn, with `farik_request_sites`: the first page you want and why, such as "the official list
of product recalls, to check this product". Your task then waits for the owner. If the owner
declines, or you cannot wait, say in your note that the recalls were not checked.

An open recall is a reason to leave the product out: never recommend a product with an open recall.
Say which recall it is, with the address of the page and its date.

## 2. The standard it must meet

Find the safety standard or the legal rule that applies where the product will be sold or used, and
name it. Examples: a child's car product is held to the rules for child restraints and car
accessories of the country, an electrical good to its electrical safety standard, a toy to the toy
safety standard. Write the standard by name and number, and the mark a compliant product carries, so
the founder can look for it on the product and its box.

## 3. What the listing shows

Compare the listing with the standard: does it name the standard, show the mark, name the maker and
give an address for it? A product with no maker, no mark or no standard named is a risk, and you
say so. Do not guess that a mark is genuine; say that the founder should ask the seller for the test
report or certificate.

## 4. What you write

For each product: the recall check and what it found or could not reach, the standard and the
mark, and your reason. This is not legal advice or a certification, and you say so.
