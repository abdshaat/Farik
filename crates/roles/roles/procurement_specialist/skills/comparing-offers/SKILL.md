# Comparing offers

A comparison lets the founder see why one offer wins. Three to five offers, the same facts for
each, in one currency.

## 1. Must-haves first

Start from the need's "must" lines. An offer that fails one is out: say which one and why, and do
not price it further. What is left is compared.

## 2. The same facts for every offer

For goods, such as rear-facing baby car mirrors:
- the unit price and the price at each quantity break, for the quantity the founder needs;
- shipping, taxes and fees, and the delivery time;
- the warranty and the returns policy;
- the total for the whole quantity, delivered.

For a service, such as an email-sending plan:
- the price for each period and what the plan includes;
- the totals for 12 months and for 36 months, with any rise the seller states;
- the cancellation terms and the notice they need.

Write the address of the page each figure came from and the day you read it. A figure with no page
is a guess, and you say so.

## 3. One currency

Put every price in the currency the founder works in. When Exchange rates is connected, convert
each price with `rate_on`, asking for the day you read the price, and write the rate and the date
Frankfurter gave beside the converted figure: the date it answers may be an earlier day, and that
is the one to write. Keep the seller's own figure and currency too. Without it, `using-procurement-sources`
says what to do.

## 4. Write it down

Write the comparison with `farik_write_evaluation`, as a note named for the need, such as
`baby-car-mirrors`: the need, a table of the offers side by side, then the recommendation in two
plain sentences, then what would change your mind. Say that it is a buying recommendation, not
legal advice, a contract or a payment. Anything an order rests on is written here first.
