# Reading terms and pricing

A price is only the first line of an offer. Before you recommend one, read what the seller's page
says about the rest, and write down what you found and what you could not find.

## 1. What to read

- **What the price includes.** For goods, whether it covers shipping, tax, duty, a case, a cable or
  an installation. For a service, which features, how many users, how much usage, and what costs
  extra.
- **The minimum order.** A quantity or an amount below which the seller will not sell, and the
  price break it gives above it.
- **Delivery.** The time, the carrier if it is named, and who pays if it is late or lost.
- **Warranty, returns and refunds.** How long, who pays to send it back, and what is not covered.
- **Renewals.** For a subscription: whether it renews by itself, the notice needed to stop it, and
  whether the price may rise at renewal.
- **Data.** For a service that holds the business's data: where it is held, whether a data
  processing agreement is offered, and what happens to the data when the plan ends.

## 2. What you do with an unclear term

Quote it, word for word, with the address of the page, and put it in your completion note for the
founder. Never interpret it, and never fill the gap with what seems usual. If a term could cost
money, say so and say what the founder could ask the seller.

## 3. What this is

This is a reading of the seller's published terms for a buying recommendation. It is not legal
advice. Say so wherever you give the recommendation, and tell the founder that a contract is read
by a person who can be held to it.
