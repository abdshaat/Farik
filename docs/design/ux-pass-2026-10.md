# UX pass, October 2026

Status: decided by the planner (Opus 5.5), 2026-10-17, on the founder's "improve the UI/UX"; the founder answers no questions on it.
Source: the note-chat2 journey of 2026-10-17 (`reports/note-chat2-journey-2026-10-17.html`, 26 screenshots in `reports/note-chat2-journey-screens/`), and the web app on `fix/stray-changes` (SPEC 0.121).
Plans: `docs/plans/standalone/ux-pass/` (README gives the order). SPEC revision 0.123.

## What the journey showed

A non-technical owner ran a real team for 1 h 42 min. The product worked; the screens made the owner read too much. On Today the team band printed each waiting agent's whole question (screen 22: Ada's line is 30 lines long in a 170 px column), "Waiting on you" grew to 13 rows in no order (screens 22, 24), eight of them the same fact ("its pull request waits for you to merge it"), and the suggested connections pushed "What moved" off the screen. The task page opened on a technical check list and a "Try 1 of 4" (screen 21). The board's Done lane ran to 20 cards, each card five lines of a narrow title plus its epic's name (screen 25). Costs repeated the agents' long lines in a "Right now" column (screen 26). The Team page and setup's team step carried paragraphs (screens 06, 19).

## Rules this pass keeps

- Non-technical owner (ADR 0016): plain words, fewer of them.
- Explanations are short bullets; field-level detail behind an ⓘ (`InfoTip`, `Bullets`; `docs/brand/brand.md` Voice).
- Muted colours, one per job; this pass adds no colour and no colour job. Group headings are headings, not coloured bands.
- 360 px phones, keyboard, screen reader, AA contrast: every change keeps the existing `@media not (min-width: 640px)` rules and is checked with `expectNoAxeViolations`.
- Not here: the stale words `fix/open-issues` (ADR 0069) fixes (the Designer's setup card `jobDesigner`, "Someone on the team reviewed it", the chat panel's line, the answered questions' header, the integration branch setting, `/chats`, the blocked task's choices on the help page, setup's spending figure). This pass edits none of their keys or components' words.

## The eight changes, most value first

### 1. One short line per agent (screens 16, 22, 24, 19)

- Before: an agent waiting on the owner shows "Waiting on you: " and the agent's whole question or reason, on Today's band and its Team card.
- After: it shows "Waiting on you"; the question itself is read once, in its row under "Waiting on you". Every agent line on the band is clamped to two lines; the whole line is the element's `title`, and a screen reader reads it whole.
- Plan 01.

### 2. "Waiting on you" grouped by what the owner does (screens 16, 22, 24)

- Before: one list of up to 13 rows in the daemon's order: questions, stuck tasks and pull requests to merge mixed; each question row prints the agent's full paragraph.
- After: the heading keeps the total ("Waiting on you (13)"); under it, three groups, each shown only when it has rows, in this order:
  - "Needs setting up (n)": the AI account's key, the app's start commands, Docker's sandbox, the Designer's browser. First, because each stops work.
  - "Needs your answer (n)": questions, stuck tasks, connector calls, posts, orders, data sources, marketing plans and budgets, document changes.
  - "Ready for you to merge (n)": finished work whose pull request waits. Three rows, then "Show all n" / "Show fewer".
- A question's or a stuck task's row says what it is about ("About “Write the project’s UI inventory”") and clamps the agent's words to three lines; the whole text is on the page its Answer opens.
- Plan 02, Task 1.

### 3. Today's quieter end (screens 15, 16)

- Before: four suggested connections, each a full card, between "Waiting on you" and "What moved".
- After: "What moved" comes first; "Suggested connections" follows it with two rows and "Show all n".
- Plan 02, Task 2.

### 4. A question answered with one press (screen 17)

- Before: pick a radio, then press "Send answer"; the agent's question in bold; a two-line "About questions" section under it.
- After: each choice the agent suggests is a button that sends that answer at once, its hint under it; "Or answer in your own words" keeps its box and "Send answer"; "Let Iris decide" stays. The question reads in normal weight, split into its paragraphs. "About questions" becomes an ⓘ beside the title, as two bullets.
- The help page of a stuck task (screen 23) is left to `fix/open-issues`, which changes its choices.
- Plan 03.

### 5. The task page says what matters first (screen 21)

- Before: the lead says "CTV-5. Iris is doing it, and Ada reviews it. Try 1 of 4."; the first tab is "Summary and checks", which opens on the task's intent (often a line meant for the agent), then the agent's note, then every check's text; the side always shows "Adding it to your project" with "Not yet: the task has not been accepted."
- After: the lead drops the try count while the task is on its first try ("Try 2 of 4." shows from the second). The tab is "Summary". It opens on the agent's latest note, then "What this task is for", then one line "1 of 2 checks passed." with each check behind "Show each check". "Adding it to your project" shows only once there is something to say (accepted, waiting to be added, added, or a private folder); its hint moves into an ⓘ. While the page loads it says "Loading…" under its back link instead of a blank page.
- Plan 04.

### 6. A denser board (screens 20, 25)

- Before: each card is a title wrapped to five lines, the epic's name on its own line(s), the id on another, the status on another; Done lists every accepted task (20 in the journey).
- After: the title is clamped to three lines; the id and the epic's name share one muted line, cut with an ellipsis; Done shows every card that waits on the owner and the five most recent others (highest task number), then "Show all n" / "Show fewer".
- Plan 05.

### 7. Costs reads as a bill (screens 13, 26)

- Before: a three-column table whose "Right now" column repeats the agents' long lines; role names on two lines under each name; a paragraph about resting; a sentence about conversations; four metrics as label/value/note lines; weeks and message counts as two more lines.
- After: two columns, agent (picture, name, role tag) and today's amount, highest first, amounts right-aligned in monospace; "Your chats with the team" is the table's last row, with an ⓘ saying they count toward the daily limit; the resting paragraph becomes an ⓘ beside "By agent", as bullets. The four metrics are four tiles (the number large, its label under it, its note behind an ⓘ); "Active weeks" and the message counts move under a "More numbers" disclosure.
- Plan 06.

### 8. Fewer words on the Team page and setup's team step (screens 06, 19)

- Before: the Team page's lead, "Pause, retire or replace" and "What a team like this costs" are paragraphs; setup's team step has a three-sentence lead, a two-sentence legend and a two-sentence "More roles" note.
- After: the Team page's lead is one line with its rules behind an ⓘ; "Pause, retire or replace" and the Designer's cost are short bullets. Setup's lead is "Six people, one for each job. Rename anyone now or later."; the legend is "Where should your team come from?"; the "More roles" note is "Tick one to add it. Seven people at most."
- Plan 07.

## Considered and left out

- Putting "Waiting on you" above the request box: `docs/design/web-ui.md` keeps the request box at the top of Today on a phone (founder-approved design); grouping and the collapsed merge group shorten the list enough.
- A spending meter on Costs: it would need a colour job the palette does not have.
- Compacting "What moved" cards: the founder chose cards with pictures (standalone plan `today-moved-cards`).
- The chat panel: its one stale line is `fix/open-issues`'s; its layout tested well in the journey (screen 18).
- Linking a merge row straight to its pull request on GitHub: `waiting.list` carries no pull request address; a wire change for it is a later step.
