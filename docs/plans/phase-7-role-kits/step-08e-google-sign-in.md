## Goal

ADR 0042 pulls Google's route forward from after the launch for one scope, `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/adwords`, so the Marketing Specialist's Google Ads connection (step 08f) signs in with Google through Farik's own Google app, with nothing pasted. ADR 0035's decided Google design holds where it applies: a Desktop client in a Google Cloud project the founder owns, PKCE, its secret treated as non-confidential, `iss` required, no revocation. What changes is where it is used: not for a Google MCP server at an address, but for Farik's own `stdio` connector, which never receives the sign-in (08f). After this step a team entry `farik connector <name>` may carry `oauth`; Farik signs it in, from the web app and from `farik connect`, with the registered app the table names for that connector, keeps, refreshes and lists it as step 03 does, and Remove says where to remove Farik in Google's settings. Out of scope: the `google-ads` connector, its name in `FARIK_CONNECTORS`, and how its calls use the grant (08f); every other Google scope and every Google MCP server (after the launch, ADR 0035); Google's verification of the scope, a launch dependency below.

## Decisions

- **Verification waits for the launch site, and the app stays in Testing** (the founder, 2026-10-05): Google's verification of the scope needs a homepage and a privacy policy on Farik's verified domain, which the web launch's website (phase 11) provides. Until then the app is in Testing, as the founder chose: the founder and named test users sign in, and sign in again every 7 days. Rejected: moving step 03e's homepage forward into phase 7.
- **What Google and GitHub say**, read 2026-10-05 by the planner and fetched again 2026-10-06 by the readiness review (each page's last update in brackets):
  - Developer tokens: "Developer tokens were sunset on September 9, 2026"; sending one "is optional and ignored by the API servers"; "Your API access levels are determined by the Google Cloud project you used to generate your OAuth credentials" (developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/api-policy/developer-token [2026-09-30]). Farik sends none.
  - Access levels: a project that enables the Google Ads API gets Test access, which reaches test accounts only; Explorer (on applying in the console, possibly granted at once) reaches production accounts with 2,880 operations a day and blocks `KeywordPlanIdeaService`, user management, account creation and billing (08f answers the keyword-ideas refusal with its Explorer sentence); Basic (15,000 a day, both kinds of account) needs the project's brand verification first and about 10 business days; Standard is unlimited after a manual audit (…/api-policy/access-levels [2026-09-30]). Every `Search` or `SearchStream` request is one operation, mutates count one per operation, and the limit is per Google Cloud project, shared by every user of Farik's app (…/best-practices/quotas [2026-09-30]).
  - Sign-in: the desktop client's loopback redirect may use `localhost` in place of `127.0.0.1`, with a path, on any port; PKCE S256; authorization `https://accounts.google.com/o/oauth2/v2/auth`, token `https://oauth2.googleapis.com/token`; revoking "removes all OAuth 2.0 scopes previously granted to a project … for all clients registered under that project" (developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/native-app [2026-09-14]). The client secret is required in practice, in the exchange and in the refresh (ADR 0035's probe: `client_secret is missing.`).
  - The scope is sensitive. Verification needs authorised domains verified in Search Console, a public homepage and a privacy policy on that domain linked from the consent screen, brand verification, a justification and a demo video, and takes 3 to 5 business days; until then the app is in Testing: a warning screen, at most 100 named test users, and refresh tokens that end after 7 days (…/production-readiness/sensitive-scope-verification [2026-08-19]).
  - GitHub's secret scanning knows Google OAuth client secrets: push protection blocks a push that holds one, and one found in a public repository is reported to Google, which may act on the client's project (docs.github.com/en/code-security/secret-scanning/introduction/supported-secret-scanning-patterns).
- **A Farik connector may sign in.** `CustomTransport::Stdio` gains `oauth: Option<OAuthSettings>`. `validate_team` keeps `oauth_on_stdio` for every `stdio` entry but the exact pair: `command` `farik` and `args` exactly two, `connector` and a word matching `^[a-z][a-z0-9-]{0,39}$` (the kit loader holds the word to `FARIK_CONNECTORS`, ADR 0038; the table matches only its own words). On the pair, `client_id` is `farik_connector_client` at `…/oauth/client_id`, and `callback_port`, with or without `client_id`, is `farik_connector_client` at `…/oauth/callback_port` alone (no `callback_port_without_client`): "farik_connector_client: Farik's own connector signs in with Farik's own app", since the app's id and port are the table's. `oauth_with_keys` holds for the pair as for any entry. `spec_sha256` adds `oauth` to a `stdio` definition only when present, so every hash kept stands. `CustomServer::oauth()` answers either transport's. The kit's `stdio` shape (`kit.rs`, `shape`) allows `oauth`. ADR 0033 says `http` only; Task 7 amends it. Rejected: an `app` field of its own on the entry, a second way of saying "signed in" (ADR 0033 chose one); and an `http` entry at a Google host, which would hand the access token to a server process.

The connector name is data until 08f ships it; nothing in this step starts it.

The authorization address carries `client_id`, `redirect_uri`, `response_type=code`, `scope` (space-joined), `state`, `code_challenge` and `code_challenge_method=S256`; no `access_type`, and no `resource`: ADR 0035 decided to send it to Google's MCP servers, but this route has no MCP resource, and Task 7 amends ADR 0035 to say so. The exchange posts `code`, `client_id`, `client_secret`, `redirect_uri`, `grant_type=authorization_code` and `code_verifier` with `Accept: application/json`. Its answer fails, each as `Failed`, when `token_type` is not `Bearer` (ignoring ASCII case: "<name> did not give Farik a bearer token"), when there is no `refresh_token` ("<name> did not give Farik a lasting sign-in"), and when `scope` lacks a scope asked for ("you did not allow Farik all it asks of <name>; sign in again and tick every box"). The grant: the entry's issuer and token endpoint, `resource` `https://googleads.googleapis.com/` (bookkeeping, as 03b keeps the server's address), the client id, both tokens, `expires_at` from `expires_in`, the granted scopes, no revocation endpoint, `app: Some("google")`. Every endpoint passes step 03's https-or-loopback check.

A grant whose `app` names no entry of the table lapses at once, with no request ("Sign in again"; on a build without Google's secret, signing in again is then `sign_in_not_supported`). `invalid_grant` lapses it, which is how a Testing app's seven-day refresh tokens end: "Sign in again" on the agent's page. **No revocation**: Remove deletes the local grant only (ADR 0035, Google's revocation ends every agent's grant at once), and its confirmation is 03b's sentence naming Google's settings.

The launch route answers a signed-in Farik connector with its command and an empty environment (08f adds its ticket), never a token; it first refreshes one that expires within 60 s (`LAUNCH_VALID_FOR`) and answers a lapsed one 403 `sign_in_again: …`. Listing its tools passes no token to the process (a `stdio` server takes no bearer). `team.get` says `auth: oauth`, `provider: "Google"`, `revokes: false`.
- **`farik connect` signs a Farik connector in too.** `keep_sign_in` takes a `stdio` server through `app_for_farik_connector` and `start_app_sign_in` with 03b's `connect_with` table (`REGISTERED_APPS` from `connect`), prints `Sign in to Google in your browser: <address>` to the error stream, opens it, waits, prints `Signed in to Google.` (03b's line for an app, which names its provider), lists the tools and keeps the grant as for `http`.
- **An attempt is bound to the whole transport.** `Attempt`'s `url` and `oauth` become `transport: CustomTransport` (`oauth` inside), and `Binding::is` compares it with the server's whole transport, so an attempt made for the pair serves neither an `http` server of the same name nor the pair with other settings.
